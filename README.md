## Contents

The Steiner problem asks for the shortest connected set containing a finite
number of given points, the mathematics behind soap films. We study the
existence of solutions beyond Euclidean geometry, in general metric spaces,
with the direct method of the calculus of variations, following L. Ambrosio
and P. Tilli, *Topics on Analysis in Metric Spaces*.

1. **The parametric approach:** existence of a curve of minimal length
   between two points (total variation, metric derivative, Arzelà–Ascoli,
   arc-length reparametrisation).
2. **Non-parametric formulation:** compact connected sets minimising the
   1-dimensional Hausdorff measure, and the equivalence between the length of
   a curve and the Hausdorff measure of its image.
3. **The Steiner problem for n points:** Steiner trees, the 120° junction
   condition, and existence of a global minimum using the Hausdorff distance
   and the Hausdorff measure.
